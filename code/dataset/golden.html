We don't grade forecasts our own way. We use the recognized format (FutureX, the benchmark MiroMind topped) and the recognized score (Brier), then point them at games whose results are already known — and let the arithmetic deliver the verdict.

FutureX template
a verifiable future event  +  a probability committed BEFORE it happens  +  automatic Brier grading AFTER it resolves. FutureX — arXiv:2508.11987. Verifiable, pre-committed, auto-graded: ungameable, no hindsight.

the score — Brier (a proper scoring rule)

Brier = ( probability you gave − what actually happened )2
0.00perfect
0.25coin-flip
1.00confidently wrong

why it can't be gamed

A proper scoring rule: your expected score is lowest only when you report your honest probability. You can't improve it by faking confidence. We pair it with calibration and CLV (closing-line value — did you have the price first?).

THE CLEAREST CASE — THE FORMULA DOES THE TALKING

Market priced Argentina at 87.1% (implied_prob_american(-675)). Argentina lost 2–1 → outcome for the favourite = 0. So brier(0.871, 0) = 0.759 — versus 0.25 for a coin-flip and 0.00 for a perfect call. The market was confidently wrong, and that is a fact the arithmetic produced.

Honest scope: this seed is an upset highlight-reel on purpose — it proves the formula discriminates right from wrong calls; it is not the market's true long-run Brier. Sources per case in seed-resolved.json.
