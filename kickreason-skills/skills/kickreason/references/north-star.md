# The North Star

**We use MiroMind to predict — but not for the final result.** We surface the IMPORTANT questions, isolate
the DEBATABLE nodes, and serve EXPERT NARRATIVE on them. The value is question-selection + reasoning on
contested nodes + a published grade — not the outcome call.

## Why (validated against real intelligence practice)
"Who wins the World Cup?" is exactly where one deep-research call just echoes the market → ~zero added value
(Value-of-Information: information is only worth paying for if it could change a decision). Professional
intelligence is **question-first**: a good Priority Intelligence Requirement "asks only one question" tied to
one decision. Intelligence failure is often a *question-selection* failure. And demand for the *debate* (not
the result) is large and proven (ESPN First Take ~517k viewers on argument; ~9% of Premier-League tweets are
about contested VAR calls; people come to media to learn *why* the odds sit where they do).

## The three pieces (operational)
- **Important question** — flip the answer and the parent forecast/decision changes materially (leverage);
  the answer is genuinely uncertain (near 50/50 carries the most information); it has a clean dated answer
  (gradeable); and research can actually move it. Not "who lifts the trophy."
- **Debatable node** — genuinely contested: sources disagree, market vs eye-test diverge, evidence cuts both
  ways. Filter out the settled. The debate itself is the value signal.
- **Expert narrative** — both cases laid out, sourced, a calibrated lean, and a published grade — the
  intel-desk standard, not a hot take.

## The honest hard parts
- One pass ≈ market consensus, even on sub-questions → the value is *selection + legibility*, not a private edge.
- Serving both sides is what the tool fights you on (deep-research agents skew one-sided) — force the
  counter-case search. Both-sides structure buys **legibility, not accuracy** — sell "you can argue with it,"
  not "it's more right."
- The champion node is the APEX the debatable rungs ladder to — don't sell it as the edge.
